Why a glass lap?

Cast iron and granite laps work, but glass does three things they don't: it starts optically flat, it resists embedding abrasive, and when it wears out of true you can re-flatten it yourself in minutes.

Flat

True from day one

Our laps are ground and checked for flatness, so your first pass is already a reference surface. No break-in lottery, no lapping a curve into your workpiece.

Rigid

Rigidity without weight

Glass is stiff in thin sections. A 1/4" lap behaves like a much thicker plate — easier to handle, easier to keep square to the work, and far lighter on the shipping bill.

Renewable

Re-flattenable forever

A lap that goes concave ruins flatness. Rub a glass lap on wet silicon carbide paper (or a coarse slurry on a partner plate) and it's flat again — a life of re-flattening, not disposal.

How to use a glass lap

1 · Load the paste

Work a small amount of lapping paste — matched to your grit stage — across the lap. For frosted laps, a few drops of light oil or water as the carrier; spread it thin and even.

2 · Figure-8 motion

Move the workpiece in overlapping figure-8 paths, covering the whole lap evenly. Light, steady pressure. Rinse and re-load as the abrasive dulls; step down grits when scratches are uniform.

3 · Keep the lap flat

Check the lap against a straightedge. If it's riding hollow, rub it face-down on wet 220-grit silicon carbide paper on a known-flat plate until it cuts evenly again. Two minutes, done.

Frequently asked questions

Why frosted glass instead of plain window glass?

A frosted (ground/etched) surface has micro-texture that holds abrasive paste and cuts fast and evenly. Plain polished glass is too slick for grinding — the paste beads and slides. That's why frosted laps do the 320–1000 grit work and clear laps do the polishing stages.

What grits should I buy?

Start at 320 if the parts need real flattening, 400–600 for general work, 800–1000 as the pre-polish step, then move to a clear lap with polishing compound. Buy the whole ladder if you can — stepping down grits on the same lap type keeps the surface consistent.

How do I know the lap is flat?

Sight along a straightedge across the face in two directions, or ink-spot two laps together and rub — even, full-coverage transfer means flat. Any low spot shows as a pattern of no-contact areas.

Can I get custom sizes and shapes?

Yes — that's the point of the configurator. Round by diameter, square by side, rectangle by length × width, in thicknesses from 1/4" to 1". Send your spec and we quote it.

Is glass fragile?

Lapping glass is ground and stress-relieved for shop use, and normal lapping pressures are far below what it takes to break it. Treat it like a good optical flat: no point impacts, no thermal shock, store it flat and padded. Edge chipping in shipping is covered — see shipping notes.

Do you sell lapping paste?

Yes, in tins matched to every grit stage, as an add-on in the configurator. Many customers buy laps and paste together so the first grit step is ready to go.

How is it shipped?

Each lap is individually sleeved and suspended in a double-boxed foam bed, edges protected. We ship domestically in a few business days; damaged in transit, we replace it, no argument.
