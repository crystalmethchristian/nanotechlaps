Why a glass lap?

Cast iron and granite laps work, but glass does three things they don't: it starts optically flat, it resists embedding abrasive, and when it wears out of true you can re-flatten it yourself in minutes.

Flat

True from day one

Every lap is ground and checked for flatness before it ships. Your first pass is already a reference surface. No break-in, no lapping a curve into your work.

Rigid

Stiff without the weight

Glass stays stiff in thin sections. A 1/4" lap holds up like a much thicker plate, it is easier to keep square to the work, and it ships lighter.

Renewable

Re-flattens, so it lasts

When a lap goes concave it stops being flat. Rub a glass lap on wet silicon carbide paper and it is flat again. You keep re-flattening it instead of throwing it out.

How to use a glass lap

Load the paste

Work a small amount of lapping paste, matched to your grit stage, across the lap. For frosted laps, a few drops of light oil or water as the carrier. Spread it thin and even.

Figure-8 motion

Move the workpiece in overlapping figure-8 paths, covering the whole lap evenly. Light, steady pressure. Rinse and re-load as the abrasive dulls; step down grits when scratches are uniform.

Keep the lap flat

Check the lap against a straightedge. If it's riding hollow, rub it face-down on wet 220-grit silicon carbide paper on a known-flat plate until it cuts evenly again. Two minutes, done.

Frequently asked questions

Why frosted glass instead of plain window glass?

A frosted (ground/etched) surface has micro-texture that holds abrasive paste and cuts fast and evenly. Plain polished glass is too slick for grinding, the paste beads and slides. That is why frosted laps do the 320–1000 grit work and clear laps do the polishing stages.

What grits should I buy?

Start at 320 if the parts need real flattening, 400–600 for general work, 800–1000 as the pre-polish step, then move to a clear lap with polishing compound. If you can, buy the whole ladder. Stepping down grits on the same lap type keeps the surface consistent.

How do I know the lap is flat?

Sight along a straightedge across the face in two directions, or ink-spot two laps together and rub. Even, full-coverage transfer means flat. Any low spot shows as a pattern of no-contact areas.

Is glass fragile?

Lapping glass is ground and stress-relieved for shop use, and normal lapping pressures are far below what it takes to break it. Treat it like a good optical flat: no point impacts, no thermal shock, store it flat and padded. Damaged in transit? We replace it — see shipping notes.
